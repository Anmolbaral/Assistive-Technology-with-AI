<InfoCard title="What is Prompt Engineering?" icon="Lightbulb">
Prompt engineering is the practice of asking AI the right question to get actionable, accurate answers. A well-crafted prompt includes context, specificity, and clear goals—turning vague requests into precise, useful recommendations.
</InfoCard>

---

## The SETT Anatomy of a Good Prompt

The **SETT Framework** (Student, Environment, Tasks, Tools) isn't just for AT decision-making—it's also the perfect template for writing AI queries.

### **S**tudent Need
Describe the challenge or learning profile (without identifying the student).  
Examples: "student with dyslexia," "5th grader with fine motor challenges," "middle school student with ADHD"

### **E**nvironment
Describe the setting and available resources.  
Examples: "general education classroom," "1:1 Chromebook environment," "resource room with iPads"

### **T**ask
What does the student need to accomplish?  
Examples: "write multi-paragraph essays," "decode multisyllabic words," "take notes during lectures"

### **T**ools (optional but helpful)
Specify tech level or cost preferences.  
Examples: "low-tech options," "free or low-cost tools," "mid-tech apps for Chromebooks"

## Examples: Bad → Good

Let's see how adding SETT elements transforms vague prompts into powerful ones.

### Example 1: Reading Support

<ProsConsCard 
  bad={{
    title: "Bad Prompt",
    example: "Help with reading",
    reason: "Too vague. What kind of reading challenge? What grade? What's the goal?"
  }}
  good={{
    title: "Good Prompt",
    example: "What low-tech AT tools help elementary students with dyslexia decode multisyllabic words during independent reading?",
    reason: "Includes student need (dyslexia, decoding), environment (elementary, independent reading), task (decode words), and tool preference (low-tech)."
  }}
/>

### Example 2: Writing Assistance

<ProsConsCard 
  bad={{
    title: "Bad Prompt",
    example: "Tools for my student",
    reason: "No context. What task? What challenge? What devices are available?"
  }}
  good={{
    title: "Good Prompt",
    example: "What are free text-to-speech tools for Chromebooks that help middle school students with reading comprehension?",
    reason: "Specifies environment (Chromebooks), student level (middle school), challenge (reading comprehension), task (implied: reading assignments), and cost (free)."
  }}
/>

### Example 3: Note-Taking

<ProsConsCard 
  bad={{
    title: "Bad Prompt",
    example: "Note taking help",
    reason: "Doesn't specify the challenge, grade level, or device constraints."
  }}
  good={{
    title: "Good Prompt",
    example: "What mid-tech and high-tech AT tools support high school students with slow processing speed in taking lecture notes on an iPad?",
    reason: "Student need (slow processing), environment (high school, iPad), task (lecture notes), tool level (mid/high-tech)."
  }}

## Quick Reference: Prompt Formula

```
What [tech level] AT tools help [grade/age] students with [challenge]
[accomplish task] in a [environment] setting?
```

**Example:**  
"What low-tech and mid-tech AT tools help 4th-grade students with dysgraphia complete written assignments in a general education classroom with Chromebooks?"
