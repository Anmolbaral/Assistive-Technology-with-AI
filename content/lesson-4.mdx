## SETT Framework Refresher

The **SETT Framework**, developed by Joy Zabala, is the gold standard for assistive technology decision-making. It ensures that AT choices are student-centered, context-aware, and purposeful.

## Walkthrough Example: 5th Grader with Dysgraphia

Let's walk through a real-world scenario using SETT + AI.

### 📋 **SETT Data Collection** (Keep confidential—don't enter into AI)

**Student:**  
- Name: [REDACTED for privacy]
- Grade: 5th
- Challenge: Dysgraphia (slow, labored, illegible handwriting)
- Strengths: Strong verbal skills, creative ideas, good reading comprehension

**Environment:**  
- General education classroom (25 students)
- 1:1 Chromebook program
- Teacher uses Google Classroom
- Peer collaboration encouraged

**Tasks:**  
- Write multi-paragraph essays (narrative, expository)
- Take notes during science and social studies
- Complete worksheets (short answer, fill-in-the-blank)

**Barriers:**  
- Handwriting is slow (5-7 words/minute vs. 20+ expected)
- Fatigue after 10 minutes of writing
- Illegible handwriting makes self-editing difficult

**Tools to Explore:**  
- Speech-to-text (dictation)
- Word prediction
- Graphic organizers
- Keyboarding supports

---

### 🤖 **AI Query** (De-identified, SETT-based)

> "What are mid-tech and high-tech AT tools for a 5th-grade student with dysgraphia who needs to write essays and take notes on a Chromebook? Looking for free or low-cost options that support planning, drafting, and editing."

---

### 💬 **Simulated AI Response**

**Answer:**  
Students with dysgraphia often benefit from speech-to-text, word prediction, and organizational supports. Here are Chromebook-compatible options by tech level.

**Recommendations:**

**Mid-Tech:**
- [Google Voice Typing](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/4492226) (built-in, free)
- [Read&Write for Google Chrome](https://www.texthelp.com/en-us/products/read-write/) (free tier available)
- [Co:Writer Universal Extension](https://www.donjohnston.com/cowriter/) (word prediction)

**High-Tech:**
- [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/) (free tier + premium)
- [Kami](https://www.kamiapp.com/) (PDF annotation + voice notes)

**Tips:**
1. Start with [Google Voice Typing](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/4492226) for dictation; teach punctuation commands ("period," "comma," "new paragraph")
2. Pair speech-to-text with a graphic organizer (like a digital story map) to help with planning before drafting
3. Collect baseline data (words per minute, writing quality) before and during the 2-week trial

**Sources:**
- [Edutopia — Free Assistive Tech Tools for Academic Success](https://www.edutopia.org/article/free-assistive-tech-tools-support-academic-success/)
- [Joy Zabala — SETT Framework & AT Resources](https://www.joyzabala.com/links-resources)

**Disclaimer:**  
Use the SETT Framework and your professional judgment. Consult your AT specialist for trials and training.

---

### ✅ **Next Steps** (Back to You)

1. **Review sources** — Click the Edutopia link, explore the tools mentioned.
2. **Consult AT specialist** — Share the list, discuss which to trial first.
3. **Trial [Google Voice Typing](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/4492226)** — Teach the student punctuation commands in a low-stakes setting (e.g., journaling).
4. **Collect data** — Track words per minute, writing quality, and student feedback for 2 weeks.
5. **Adjust** — If Voice Typing works well, add it to the IEP. If not, trial [Read&Write for Google Chrome](https://www.texthelp.com/en-us/products/read-write/) next.
